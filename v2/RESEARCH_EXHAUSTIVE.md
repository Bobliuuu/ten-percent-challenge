## 1. Extreme compression 2023-2026 (item 1 of the brief)

What changed vs QUANT.md (which already covers QTIP, AQLM, QuIP#, GPTAQ, GuidedQuant, trellis, Leech):
- **Lookup-free trellis codes, Proteus** (ICML 2026, poster 63948; alphaxiv 2610.09212): integer-mixing generator + affine Gaussianiser, per-layer candidate pool, optional few-shot distillation of affine statistics only (skip that: gray). Reports better PPL/zero-shot than QTIP at 2 bits on Llama-2 7B-70B (abstract-level). A later preprint names Proteus and QTIP as the leading 2-bit weight quantisers (unverified). Use: swap into `v2/vq/tcq.py` as the code generator; removes codebook bytes (CODING s6).
- **Lattice**: LLVQ Leech-lattice (2603.11021, "Hadamard-free" PTQ + GPTQ-style Hessian correction, parallel dequantisation, no codebook materialisation); UniSVQ (2606.10520, integer-lattice affine codewords); HyperQuant (2606.23406, optimal low-dim lattices + Rice-coded indices, beats HIGGS at 3-5 bps, data-free). A community report (unverified) of a Leech-lattice 2-bit run on Qwen3-4B shows a 14.7 pt MMLU drop: 2 bpw is still a wall below about 8B.
- **GPTQ-family fixes**: residual-error compensation (2604.07955), D2Quant (2602.02546), BPDQ (2602.04163), CCQ convolutional codes (2507.07145). All closed-form.
- **Data-free + entropy coding**: Float8@2bits (2601.22787): ANS over fine-grained scalar quantisation, rate-distortion with an l1 entropy proxy. Radio (2505.03031) rate-distortion optimisation. These are the same rate trick we have as ECVQ (QUANT #4) but data-free.
- **1-bit PTQ**: AF1 (2609.06161) claims a true 1.0 bpw counting all overhead (hidden overhead in earlier binarisers), 90% memory reduction, perplexity-level evidence only.
- **Expert pruning/merging (MoE)**: REAP (2510.13999): prune by router-weighted activation norm, near-lossless coding at 50% on Qwen3-Coder-480B/Kimi-K2, merging underperforms pruning on generation. Unified scoring formulation (2606.15716, EMNLP Findings 2026, code `ZongfangLiu/unified-expert-pruning`): task-agnostic -> gate-free routed-token-averaged score; task-specific -> routing frequency + gate weight is fine if calibration matches. EASY-EP (domain demonstrations, router weight x output magnitude x token importance), "Half the Experts, All the Code" (2607.16721): half the experts, HumanEval+ unchanged, MBPP+ -4.5 on one family. OMP-MoE (2609.31631): 93.3% of baseline average at 50% on Qwen3-30B-A3B. SHAPE (2606.09886), AIMER (2603.18492, calibration-free), ConMoE (2605.29350), MoE Pathfinder (2512.18425). Rival #4's own rule (routing mass on unlabeled text) is the weakest of these; REAP/EASY-EP-style scores are cheap CPU-side changes.
- **Expert decomposition**: D2-MoE (2502.17298), GS-MoE (2610.32009), MoBE; the Tucker alternative degraded on Qwen3-30B-A3B (2609.06076, "cost-normalised study of post-compression adjustment" - read it before spending on delta methods).
- **Layer dropping**: ShortGPT (BI metric), LaCo, Prune&Comp (2507.18212), FlattenGPT (ICML 2026). The reported accuracy retention is task-average; nobody reports per-item agreement. Our X07 TALE already targets agreement.
- **Low-rank / sparse + quant**: CALDERA (<2.5 bpw), SLiM (2:4 + 4-bit), SVD-LLM (2403.07378) + 2-bit. Only low-rank as pure replacement of a matrix (no extra adapter on top) is R4-clean; its quality at 90% byte cut is poor.
- **Weight sharing**: Basis Sharing (2410.03765, ICLR 2025): shared SVD bases across layers, -25% perplexity vs SVD baselines at equal size; q/k/v/up/gate share, o_proj/down only SVD. Training-free. Useful only as a pre-step that lowers the effective rank before VQ; no 90% claim.
- **Delta coding / model-collection tricks** (BitDelta 2402.10193, DeltaZip, Delta-CoMe, DARE): compress a fine-tune against its base. Does NOT apply to a single model unless the start model is itself an upcycled MoE or a fine-tune whose base ships for free (base is not shippable for free: R1 counts everything). Only useful inside one file: upcycled MoE experts (Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, Qwen2-57B-A14B are upcycled from dense; Mixtral partially) vs a shared expert base, as D2-MoE does.
- **Hashing / codebook tricks**: nothing newer than AQLM/GPTVQ that fits a 4-day window.
- **Lossless**: DFloat11 (2504.11651, Huffman on bf16 exponents, about 30%, bit-exact), ZipNN, ECF8 (2510.02676), NeuZip. Applies only to tensors we keep in bf16/fp32 (norms, router, any 16-bit attention). The ZipNN comparison in 2608.25188 reports quantised checkpoints barely shrink.
- **Tile-level ANS** reaches within 0.01-0.1 bits of the Shannon limit on low-bit formats (survey 2608.25188): so decoder complexity is not a reason to avoid rANS.

## 2. Native low-bit models (item 2)
- **Bonsai (PrismML)**: 1-bit Bonsai 27B 3.9 GB at 1.125 bpw (about 7.2% of 54 GB), ternary Bonsai 27B 5.9 GB at 1.71 bpw (10.9%); both are quantisations of Qwen3.6-27B (not a new pretrain); the vendor reports 89.5% / 94.6% of fp16 average over 15 thinking-mode benchmarks (vendor numbers, no third-party evaluation found). Four dense Bonsai sizes 1.7B-27B exist; Bonsai 27B has 29.66% zeros (third-party layout paper). Per-item agreement with the parent is far below benchmark-average retention (our own 8B finding in SYNTHESIS: 88.9% vs parent).
- **BITCOS** (2609.16338): bitmap + sign layout, 2-z bits per weight, break-even z>=0.415; smaller than 5-trit packing in 26/29 ternary models; sparsest model 1.485 bpw. For us: only a layout lever when z>0.415; ANS on trits dominates it (entropy H(z) < 2-z for all z; at z=0.30 H=1.581 vs BITCOS 1.70).
- **Falcon-E** (TII, 1B-3B, bitnet.cpp), **BitNet b1.58 2B4T** (2504.12285, 2.4B, 4T tokens), DeepGrove Maple 20B-A1B ternary reasoning model, TriLM/Spectra: all below the interesting size or with a vocab-embedding problem (128k x 2560 fp16 embedding alone is 13.7% of the 2B4T fp16 size, so embeddings must be quantised/trimmed in the shipped file).
- **Qwen-derived ternary PTQ** (CAT-Q, ICML 2026, unverified).
Where it fits: lane A, still gated on the unruled base-identity question (RULES s5). Idea 6 above is the only new lever (CPU byte audit).

## 3. Training-free editing and feedback (item 3)
Binding constraint (RULES 4b-4d): feedback must change the model's own computation, new class must be generated end to end, no row-fitted classifier.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| AlphaEdit (2410.02355) | project update onto null space of old-key covariance | yes | best neighbour protection | leakage non-zero and growing (BetaEdit 2605.09285, reproduction 2606.26783); "Labyrinth and the Thread" reframes it as constrained LS; CrispEdit (curvature-aware) |

| Soft recursive least squares (2601.15686) | sequential LS update with soft preservation | yes | good for many sequential feedback batches | |
| Steer2Edit (2602.09870, code) | steering vector -> component-level rank-1 edits (heads, MLP neurons), closed form | yes | diffuse, in-weights, no hook | evaluated on safety/truthfulness/length, not classification; adapt by taking the steering vector = mean hidden of annotated-correct minus model-wrong |

| GRACE/WISE/MELO/MEND/SERAC | side memory / trained editor | no | NO (R4) | excluded |
| "LLM writes weights from a natural-language goal" (weight-space-programming posts, Nov 2026, secondary sources only) | LLM edits tensors | gradient-free | needs a big model in the loop; fault removal only, cannot add abilities per the same posts | not usable: adds an outside model |
Design notes distilled (for lane F):
1. Do not touch lm_head rows: set the edit target to the EXISTING unembedding direction of the new label's first token and solve for a diffuse change in layers L-4..L in down_proj (3-5 layers, Hessian-metric min-norm as already planned). This is idea 9 in SYNTHESIS, now with an AlphaEdit-style null space of the covariance of OLD-class last-token keys built from the annotation-free calibration text (not from the exam).
2. Edit down_proj, not o_proj (practitioner report: 72 vs 6 pt; unverified but consistent with the knowledge-neuron/FFN-as-memory literature).
3. Expect a weak few-label regime (DistilBERT public experiment: 45-52% from up to 640 labels vs LoRA 58% at 10): promise teach gain only after the dev gate G-F.
4. Controls for R6 are cheap in a closed form: shuffled labels flip the edit direction; empty feedback is the identity (byte-identical file).
5. Persistence of edits under later fine-tuning (EMNLP 2026 paper, 2608.24988) is irrelevant (we never train) but shows the edit is not removed by later changes: a plus for "learn and keep".

## 4. Faithfulness / retention metrics and calibration-free compression (item 4)
- Flips (Dutta et al., "Accuracy is not all you need", 2407.09141): percentage of items whose correctness changes between base and compressed; up to 15% flips with negligible accuracy change; KL divergence correlates with flips; harder tasks flip more (MMLU 8-16% at W4A4 BnB vs PIQA).
- 2609.07664 (divergence-based fidelity: flips = flip-to-wrong + flip-to-correct, TVD, JSD) and 2606.19558 ("Displacement is not direction": flips extended to coding, tool calling, instruction following, math; tests whether a fidelity metric picks the better of two near-baseline quantisations). Use for `harness` metric reporting and for choosing among best-of-N on KL-proxy (our idea 3/7 in SYNTHESIS). Clinical logit fingerprinting (PMC13274300): flip vs its own FP16 baseline of 21% while accuracy rose after NF4 (shows mean accuracy is the wrong number; consistent with R2).
- R2's metric is "retention of base-correct" = 1 - (flip-to-wrong / base-correct). Flip-to-correct are free and are NOT counted; so a metric on `flip-to-wrong only` per kind is the right dev target, not total flips. (Small point; check `harness` already does this.)
- Adversarial caveat: LLMQuA (WWW 2026) shows quantisation can silently re-program a moderation model (moderation accuracy -67.9% on correctly classified items, utility barely changed). It is a supply-chain attack, not our setting, but shows per-kind retention on safety classes is exactly where silent damage hides: keep per-kind cells for the "harmful" class.
- Calibration-free: HIGGS, HyperQuant, Float8@2bits, AIMER (experts), RTN (rival). Calibration-free means R3's "never calibrated on" holds for every source automatically.
