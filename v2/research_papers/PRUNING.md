# PRUNING: task-aware, dynamic and structured pruning literature scan (2026-11-08, research only, $0)

Scope: what can buy bytes (rule 2) or retention (rule 2: >=98% of base-correct, >=95% per class) with NO weight training, real label output, on Qwen2.5-14B/32B at <=10% of fp16. Context read first: GOAL_README.md rules 1-9, v2/PRUNE_LEGAL.md, v2/TASK_SEARCH.md (32B scalar-GPTQ at 10.03%: NEWS .961, INJ .957, ROUTE .947), v2/prune/RESULTS.md (14B MMLU: dropping 2 tail layers -0.4pt for -4% bytes; 4 tail layers -1.6pt for -8%; FFN-neuron prune -2.5..-4pt at keep .9; head prune .875 -3pt). Numbers below are from the papers' text as fetched; "(unverified)" marks things read only from search snippets or recalled, not from the paper.

## 0. Bottom line
1. Pruning only helps rule 2 as a **byte-swap**: bytes freed by removal are re-spent as extra bits/weight on the rest. At 32B, 1 of 64 layers = 1.56% of bytes; our quantizer moves ~+1 to +12 points over 10.0 -> 12.5% (TASK_SEARCH), i.e. very roughly +0.3 to +0.5 pt retention per 1% of bytes freed, noisy (+-2 pt draw noise). So a removal is only worth it if it costs **less than ~0.3 pt of base-agreement per 1% of bytes**. Tail-layer drops measured at 14B (-0.4pt/-4%, then -1.6/-8%) are right at break-even; task-aware selection is the way to push below it.
2. **Dynamic / prompt-conditioned pruning (IFPruning, GRIFFIN, Deja Vu, CATS/TEAL) does not shrink stored bytes** (all weights must be shipped; they only skip compute). They are useless for rule 2 as such. The only adaptable piece is the *static, per-task* mask (IFPruning Sec 4.3 per-task mask), which equals ordinary task-calibrated static structured pruning.
3. The metric that matters is **flip rate / agreement**, not accuracy. "Accuracy is Not All You Need" (arXiv 2407.09141): at MMLU, GPTQ-W4 changes accuracy <1pt but flips 4-8% of answers; layer-drop, Wanda and SliceGPT show flips rising ~linearly with sparsity while accuracy barely moves, SliceGPT flips even at tiny sparsity; flips concentrate on low top-margin items; KL to the base correlates with flips at Spearman 0.981. Consequence: every published "accuracy preserved" claim below is NOT evidence of >=98% agreement. Any selection must optimize agreement on base-correct items (or KL / margin-weighted loss), not accuracy.
4. Pruning + 2-bit "compounding" evidence is thin and mostly negative: nobody reports a training-free layer-prune + sub-2-bit pipeline holding 98% agreement. See section 3.
5. "MMP": no LLM-pruning method with that exact acronym found (section 4).

## 2. Task-specific pruning specifically
- TALE is the only paper doing per-task *layer* search with a labelled val set; DIET/dual-source/GISP do per-task *channel* importance from ~100 samples. All use accuracy or task loss, none agreement with the base, none test shift between source A and B for the chosen mask. Our S1 (calibrate) vs S3/S2 (held-out source) design in PRUNE_LEGAL is stricter than anything published; there is no published number for the distribution-shift penalty of a task-fitted mask. Treat as unknown, measure.
- No paper found titled "compact LLMs for a single task" with a training-free recipe that reports >=98% agreement. Task-specific compression in the literature almost always adds fine-tuning/distillation, which rule 4 forbids.

## 3. Pruning + low-bit compounding
- 2406.17415: layer drop and quantization are alternatives; crossover around 2-3 bits means at our ~1.6 bpw regime, removal *is* competitive with bits (consistent with byte-swap logic in section 0).
- MKA (2406.16330): ShortGPT 16-20 layers + GPTQ/AWQ 4-bit -> MMLU ~26 (chance), vs 55-64 for merging. Heavy removal + quantization compounds destructively.
- 2602.07804: ordering matters (LLaMA-2-7B, 4-bit): quantize-then-remove-3-layers PPL 14.93 vs remove-then-quantize 15.40; 6 layers 18.58 vs 19.77. i.e. choose layers *after* the quantizer so the selection sees quantization error (cheap to do in our pipeline).
- Budget-aware pipeline (2608.30076, unverified): 3 of 32 layers + AWQ costs 4.1% ARC-C, 6.6% throughput.
- 2606.07819 (joint structural pruning + mixed-precision, ultra-low bit): reported PPL gains vs weight-only quant but it is a learned/search method; training status unverified.
- OBR 2509.11177: error compensation between pruning and quantization, W4A4KV4 + 50% sparsity. 4-bit, not 2-bit.
- Nothing found that pairs pruning with 2-bit at >=98% agreement. Our own data: v2/prune says the quant+removal frontier was never measured (p2/p3 never finished). That unfinished frontier is the actual gap.

## 4. What "MMP" could be
No LLM pruning/compression method named "MMP" turned up in several searches (standard and extended). Nearest hits, none confirmed to be what was meant:
- MoP, Mixture of Pruners (2602.06127): depth+width, uses LoRA recovery, so not legal for us.
- MRP, Maximum Redundancy Pruning (layerwise sparsity allocation, 2503.18377, unverified).
- "Joint Structural Pruning and Mixed-Precision Quantization" (2606.07819): a "mixed-precision + pruning" reading of MMP.
- MKA / "Pruning via Merging" (2406.16330, manifold-aligned layer merging): a "model merging and pruning" reading; it beats ShortGPT under GPTQ/AWQ (MMLU 55-64 vs ~26 at heavy removal) but is a merge, ie weight arithmetic, no training; code not checked.
- Others in the list (Matching Pursuit, multi-modal pruning) have no LLM-pruning paper with that name found. If the term came from a person or a repo note, get the source; I could not resolve it.

## 5. Ranked experiments (all on dev data, S1 calibration, S3/S2 probes held out; no training; legal per v2/PRUNE_LEGAL.md rows 1-3)
Shared protocol: objective = agreement with base over **base-correct** items (hard) plus mean KL / margin-weighted flips on low-margin items (soft), never raw accuracy; choose structure AFTER fixing the quantizer recipe (2602.07804 ordering); report per-class and S3/S2 retention.

**E1. Agreement-targeted TALE on tail/late layers (32B first, 14B second).**
What: from TALE, greedy single-layer removal over the last ~24 layers of Qwen2.5-32B (64 layers), objective = retention of base-correct on S1-dev (500-1500 items, 6 classes balanced, hold out S3), stop at k in {2,4,6,8}; each removal frees 1.56%; re-run the repo quantizer at the freed bit budget (target_ratio fixed at 0.100 including the extra bits). Compare to the same k tail-forced and BI-ranked.
Expected gain: +0.5 to +1.5 pt retention at 10.0% (6 layers = 9.4% bytes ~ moving 10.0 -> ~11% quantizer equivalent = +1 to +2 pt on TASK_SEARCH curve, minus removal cost ~0.5-1 pt since label-token argmax is pruning-friendly per 2603.24652 and TALE shows tolerance of 2-6 layers). Net uncertain given +-2 pt draw noise; to pass 98% from .957 needs more.
Cost: ~1-3 GPU-h on A100/H100 for forward-only search with cached prefix (INJ/NEWS prompts short), plus one quantizer run (~$10-30 given existing pipeline). Risk: medium-high (search overfit to S1; mid-layer picks like v2/prune n=6 [22,28] collapse -> restrict to last third). Legality: rules 4/7/9 clean (original computation, real label output); different-distribution check: select with S1 only, report S3/S2; if layer set differs by source, reject. Reject any set that uses label text beyond the standard chat-template stream (as PRUNE_LEGAL).

**E2. Prune-then-quantize frontier (finish the never-run p2/p3 for 32B/14B on label tasks).**
What: grid {0, 2, 4, 6 tail layers} x {1.5, 1.6, 1.7, 1.8 bpw body} with the true allocator, 3 quantizer seeds, on INJ/NEWS dev. Output a retention-vs-bytes frontier showing whether removal beats bits at our operating point (2406.17415 says crossover ~2-3 bits).
Expected gain: informational; locates the best k; probable +0.5 to +1 pt at the best point. Cost ~$30-60. Risk: low (just measurement); noise needs 3 seeds. Legality: clean.

**E3. Static task+neutral calibrated FFN-channel prune at 10-15% (FLAP/DIET style), per-layer adaptive, with merged-source masks.**
What: score intermediate channels by FLAP fluctuation or Wanda-sp activation x weight norm on neutral text (50%) + S1 task stream (50%), DIET-style merge across 2 sources, prune 10-15% of MLP channels in later layers, no LS reconstruction (repo showed LS recon unreliable at mild pruning), then quantize. MLP is ~87% of Qwen2.5-32B layer bytes so 10% of channels = ~8.7% bytes freed.
Expected gain: label tasks use a narrower function than MMLU (where repo saw -2.5..-4pt at keep .9), so cost may be 0.3-1.5 pt of agreement for ~8% bytes = break-even to +0.5 pt. Honestly marginal; main value is if the narrow task makes it near-free. Cost ~$20-40. Risk: high on different-distribution (PRUNE_LEGAL row 2: RISK). Legality: LEGAL if calibration disclosed with neutral text; must show S3/S2 retention >= S1 retention. GRIFFIN's flocking result suggests prompt-level neuron sets are stable, which argues the task-level set is stable too.

**E4. Block-level (sublayer) agreement search with MLP-aware cost (FinerCut/BlockPruner re-targeted).**
What: candidate set = attention or MLP sublayers of the last 24 layers; cost-aware greedy picking maximum bytes freed per agreement point lost (attention 13% of a layer's bytes, MLP 87%), using KL-to-base on S1 plus base-correct flip count. Expectation from FinerCut: attention sublayers are the cheapest to drop (34/80 in Llama3-70B at 99%) but free little in GQA, so the realistic yield is ~6-10 attention drops = ~1% bytes only; the question is whether a few deep MLP drops are cheaper than whole-layer drops.
Expected gain: +0.2 to +0.8 pt over E1 (finer granularity); cost ~2-4 GPU-h; risk medium; legality clean. Run only after E1 shows layer removal pays.

**E5. Generic vocabulary trim (embedding + lm_head), pre-registered rule.**
What: the 152k x 5120 embedding and lm_head are ~3.1 GB of 65.5 GB fp16 for 32B (4.7%) if both stored; trimming to token id < 65536 + specials (label-free) frees ~2.7% of fp16 = a free ~+0.3 to +0.5 pt on the quantizer curve, at no change to body behaviour. This is PRUNE_LEGAL's gray-legal row: cutoff fixed before looking at labels, report label-token coverage, keep free generation, measure OOD (non-English/emoji) cost.
Expected gain: +0.3 to +0.6 pt; cost ~$5 (reuse E2 harness); risk: rule 9 / maintainer shift if inputs use rare tokens (byte-fallback re-split changes behaviour on non-English text); legality gray as already ruled. Not a published method; included because it is the cheapest byte source.

Not recommended: IFPruning/GRIFFIN/Deja Vu (no stored-byte saving; IFPruning trains the predictor), LLM-Streamline/MoP/Minitron/LLM-Pruner recovery (training, rule 4), SliceGPT (redundant with Hadamard rotation, high flips), unstructured SparseGPT/Wanda (zeros fight the low-bit grid; 2:4 not a byte format).

## 6. Evidence on flips/agreement after pruning (collected)
- 2407.09141 (Llama2/Yi, MMLU): GPTQ W4A16 flips 8.26/6.49/4.26% (7B/13B/70B) with accuracy delta -0.58/-0.14/-0.45; AWQ W4 7.50/6.36/4.05%; BnB W4A4 10.65/8.09/5.65%; GPTQ W8A16 negligible. Layer-drop flips rise ~linearly with dropped layers while accuracy stays flat (secondary reports cite >=25% flips at heavy dropping, unverified); Wanda flips rise steadily; SliceGPT flips large at tiny sparsity. Correct answers have higher top margin (0.758 vs 0.434 on 70B). Our own TASK_SEARCH confirms flips are margin-driven but even high-margin items flip at 4-18%.
- No paper reports >=98% base-correct retention for pruning plus sub-3-bit quantization on a classification task. The 98%/95% bar is not met by any published method in this scan; every number above is accuracy or perplexity.
