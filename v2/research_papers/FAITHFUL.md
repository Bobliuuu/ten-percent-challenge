# FAITHFUL: preserving the base model's per-item decisions under quantization

Scope: research only ($0, no code run). Question: how to push the flip rate on base-correct items from ~4.3% (Qwen2.5-32B, INJ-real, 10.03%) to <=2% (rule 2: >=98% overall, >=95% per class). Evidence labels: [V] = confirmed from a search result this session; [K] = from my background knowledge of the paper, numbers not re-verified; [H] = my hypothesis/inference. Our own numbers come from `v2/TASK_SEARCH.md` and `v2/SOLUTIONS.md` (32B INJ-real retention 0.957; 405B INJ 90.2% GEN vs 99.7% TF; two draws of one recipe differ ~2 pt; retention by base margin on 32B: [10,12) 0.960, [6,8) 0.798).

## 1. What the literature says about flips

- **"Accuracy is Not All You Need"** (Dutta, Krishnan, Kwatra, Ramjee; Microsoft; NeurIPS 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09141 [V]. Defines a *flip* as an answer that changes between correct/incorrect (and, by their count, any change) versus the baseline. Accuracy within <=2% of baseline while flips >=5%; up to 13.6% flips for quantized Llama2/Yi chat; every scheme except GPTQ W8A16 flips a lot. KL divergence over option probabilities correlates with flips (secondary source quotes Spearman ~0.96-0.97; not verified). Adding Gaussian noise to weights reproduces the flip behaviour, i.e. flips are generic approximation noise, not a method artefact. Takeaway for us: the paper's own objective-of-choice is KL to the base distribution, so KL-to-base is the right *proxy loss* to optimise or to select on.
- **Follow-ups (2026)**: "Accuracy is Not Enough: A Divergence-Based Approach to Evaluate Fidelity Loss in Quantized LLMs" https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07664 and "The Illusion of Equivalency" https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08734 [V, abstracts only]. Use JSD/TVD/flip; one reports flips can be 0% on some benchmarks even with nonzero JSD, which says flips are a thresholded (margin-dependent) function of divergence, consistent with our margin table.
- **Self-calibration** (Williams, Chrysostomou, Aletras, NAACL 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17170 [V]. Model-generated calibration text is as good as or better than real web text; random-vocabulary data is worst in every setting. Supports: calibration distribution matters, and base-model-generated (self-distilled) inputs are an accepted calibration source.
- **GPTAQ, asymmetric calibration** (Li et al., ICML 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02692 [V]. Finetuning-free GPTQ extension: each quantized layer is fit to the *full-precision model's* layer output given the already-quantized upstream, not to its own quantized-path input. ~20 lines on top of GPTQ; helps at 2-4 bit. This is exactly "error-propagation-aware, base-outputs-as-targets" and stays closed-form. A related line (QEP, tunable alpha for error propagation) and ICLR 2026 "Rethinking Residual Errors in Compensation-based LLM Quantization" (compensation-aware error term) https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.07955 [V, summaries].
- **Fisher / loss-aware grids**: LeanQuant https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10032 (loss-error-aware grid using inverse diagonal Hessian) [V]; C-PTQ (Fisher-weighted channel sensitivity, 2026) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21076 [V]; SqueezeLLM (Fisher-based sensitivity-weighted non-uniform codebooks, Kim et al. 2024) [K]. Standard GPTQ's Hessian is the layer-input Gram matrix 2XX^T (https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17323 [V]); it knows nothing about which outputs matter for the final decision. Fisher/Gauss-Newton weighting of the output (logit-margin) loss is the "decision-aware" Hessian we want.
- **Rounding learning**: AdaRound (Nagel 2020) https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10568, BRECQ (Li 2021, block-wise reconstruction), QDrop https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05740 [V/K]. AdaRound learns a per-weight up/down rounding variable by gradient on a layer reconstruction loss (weights stay on the fixed grid); BRECQ/QDrop do the same per block, QDrop adds activation-quant dropout (vision-era, little LLM relevance because we quantize weights only). MQBench describes them as "fine-tune the weight by reconstructing output" [V], which is how a hostile reader will classify them.
- **OmniQuant** (Shao 2023) [K]: *trains* learnable weight clipping + equivalent-transformation scales by block-wise gradient descent (about 1-2 h for 7B-70B on one GPU). That is gradient training of (non-weight) scales on data. Not a pure-math method.
- **Training-heavy extreme compression** (not legal for us, listed to calibrate expectations): QuIP# (fine-tunes continuous params), AQLM + PV-Tuning https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/94666 [V] (updates discrete codes and continuous params with gradients; Pareto-optimal at 2 bit). Their quality at ~2 bpw is partly bought by end-to-end KL fine-tuning, which rule "can't train the weights" excludes. Our VQ-D8 (C07/C11) is the closest legal sibling.
- **Seed/rotation variance**: SpinQuant https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16406 [V] reports best vs worst random rotation differing by up to 13 points downstream and random-Hadamard variance up to ~6 points at W4A4 (activation quant included; ours is weight-only so smaller). I found *no* paper that studies best-of-N seed selection with held-out selection; SpinQuant fixes variance by learning rotations (training). So best-of-N is under-explored, and its selection-bias behaviour must be modelled by us (section 3).
- **Bias / norm-statistics correction**: no LLM-specific LayerNorm-statistics paper found. Classical bias correction (Nagel 2019 DFQ, Banner ACIQ) subtracts the expected output shift E[Wq x - W x]; GPTAQ's closed-form residual compensation subsumes most of its effect inside a layer [H].

## 2. Why flips behave as they do (theory + our data)

Let m be the base margin (top-1 minus competitor logit) for an item and d the quantization-induced change of that margin, d ~ N(mu_item, sigma^2) approximately (Gaussian-weight-noise reproduction in Dutta et al. supports this). Flip prob = Phi(-(m - mu)/sigma). Flip rate = integral over the margin density. Consequences [H, consistent with our tables]:
1. Flips are a **tail phenomenon**: the density of items with m < ~2 sigma sets the flip rate. Our pooled 32B table (TASK_SEARCH s3) shows exactly this: bins >=10 keep 0.96, [6,8) 0.80. Median margin 10-12 does not help because the flip mass is in the thin low-margin tail, not at the median.
2. Because it is a Gaussian tail, flip rate is **super-linear in sigma**: for a tail fraction fixed, cutting sigma by ~25-30% roughly halves the flip rate when the relevant margin sits 1.5-2.5 sigma out [H]. Hence 4.3 -> 2.0 needs roughly a 25-35% reduction of the logit-margin perturbation, not 2x. That is the single most useful budget number: it is within reach of "better objective" methods (they typically cut layer reconstruction error by 20-40% over plain GPTQ at the same bits [K]) but not of small tweaks.
3. **mu_item matters, not just sigma**: systematic shifts (bias) move whole classes. Our per-class failures (S3_injection 81.3, Technology 0.90, S2_benign) look like class-conditional mean shift, i.e. the quantized model drifts toward one label. That is exactly what class-balanced calibration and bias/prior correction target [H].
4. **Why bigger is not better at equal bpw in our data (405B 9.8% vs 32B 4.3%)**: (a) 14B->32B helped (+4 to +20 pt), so within the sweet range size does help; the 405B point also pays for (b) GEN vs TF: its TF 99.7 vs GEN 90.2 says the decision is made by an autoregressive label string over several tokens, so one first-token drift cascades (flips counted at sequence level, not at one token); (c) per-layer noise accumulates over 126 layers (error propagation, the GPTAQ/QEP motivation) while the margin does not grow with depth; (d) calibration per layer on Hessians of 16k-dim inputs from ~1k calibration rows is statistically thinner (more parameters per calibration token), and (e) scale-accounting tricks to hit 9.6% may have forced fewer bits on sensitive tensors [H, unverified]. Treat (b) and (c) as the leading explanations; the experiment in section 4 (#2, #4) tests them.
5. Flip counts are **not independent across bit rates** (same items) and draw noise is about +-2 pt (TASK_SEARCH s5), the same size as the gap we want to close. Any claim below 2 pt needs multi-draw evidence.

## 3. Legality analysis (rules 4 "no new computation fitted to data" and README "can't train the weights")

Reference point: the repo's own GPTQ/VQ quantizer already fits quantized values to a calibration set via a Hessian. So the question is not "data touches the compression" but "is anything *trained* or *added*".
- **Pure-math objective changes** (Fisher/margin-weighted Hessian, asymmetric GPTAQ targets, per-layer bit allocation from sensitivity, class-balanced calibration texts, norm/bias correction folded into existing tensors): same class as GPTQ. No gradient descent, no new modules. **Low risk.** The base model's outputs used as targets are computed by the original model on calibration text; they are calibration statistics, not labels, and are never shipped. Caveat for rule 4 in the maintainer's reading: anything shipped that stores calibration-derived tables (not weights) would be "side memory"; closed-form compensation is baked into the weights, so it ships nothing extra. Adding a bias to layers that had none adds a few bytes and a new parameter; Qwen2.5 already has q/k/v biases, so only fold into those or into norm weights to avoid any "new part" argument.
- **AdaRound/BRECQ/AdaQuant rounding optimisation**: gradient-trained *discrete rounding choices* on a fixed grid, no new weights values outside the grid and no new parameters shipped. Mathematically GPTQ is also a rounding-choice optimiser (greedy + closed-form update). The honest reading: it is a more thorough optimiser of the same objective, but it uses SGD with a loss on data, and the README sentence "You can't train the weights" is broad. **Gray, medium-high risk.** I would only use it if (i) the objective is pure reconstruction/KL of the base's own outputs on calibration text, not task labels, and (ii) we get a maintainer clarification (a public issue asking "is learned rounding on a fixed grid, with no new parameters, allowed?") before relying on it.
- **OmniQuant (learned clipping/equivalent-transform scales)**: gradient-trained scales/shifts; the shifts are new tensors fused into weights. **High risk**; skip.
- **PV-Tuning/QuIP# fine-tuning, end-to-end KL distillation to the base**: clearly weight training. **Illegal.**
- **Best-of-N draw selection**: the output is one ordinary quantized model; N draws differ only in random Hadamard signs. Selecting among them on a dev set is model *selection*, like picking hyper-parameters. Rule 3 forbids the *exam* (and the 500-item unseen-source block) from influencing choice, so the selection set must be disjoint from both. **Low-to-gray risk** (a strict reader may say "computation fitted to data", but it adds no computation). Document it as a hyper-parameter search in the repro.
- **Self-distilled calibration data** (base model generates the text, base logits are targets): same status as Self-calibration (NAACL 2025) and GPTAQ. Legal as calibration math as long as no labelled exam or feedback item is used and the texts are not shipped (or if shipped, count in size and are not a lookup the system consults at inference).
- **Ensembling / averaging multiple quantized models**: inference-time ensemble multiplies size; not legal under the 10% cap. "Ensemble of roundings" is only legal if collapsed into one tensor (averaging dequantized weights leaves the grid, so it must be re-quantized, which defeats it).

### Best-of-N selection bias (important)
With N draws each having true flip rate f_i = f + e_i (e_i draw noise, sd s ~ 1 pt on the 2-pt spread we measured) and evaluated on n selection items, the winner's *observed* rate is biased low by about s_eff * E[max of N standard normals] where s_eff^2 = s^2 + sampling variance (p(1-p)/n_base-correct). For N=8, E[max] ~ 1.42; with s_eff ~ 1 pt the winner looks ~1.4 pt better than it truly is *on the selection set*, but its true advantage over a random draw is only the part due to e_i, i.e. s^2/s_eff * 1.42 ~ about 1.0-1.3 pt when sampling noise is small (n_base-correct >= 3000) and drops fast when n is small. [H, standard order-statistics argument.] Therefore: select on a set of >=3000 base-correct items disjoint from calibration, from the *same sources* as exam but different items, report the winner on a second untouched dev set, and never quote the selection-set number. Using a margin-weighted agreement score (below) rather than raw flips lowers sampling variance.

## 4. Ranked top-5 experiments (Qwen2.5-14B / 32B, INJ-real + NEWS dev)

All use the existing repo quantizer, the 1566-item INJ-real dev set (C11/C03 comparison basis) and `task_search/NEWS` dev. Evaluate GEN (real label generation), not TF. Always >=3 draws per config to see draw noise; report mean and worst class cell. Baselines for all: plain GPTQ/VQ-D8 at 10.0% on the same items.

1. **Best-of-N draw selection with disjoint selection set (N=8-16) + margin-weighted agreement score.** What: quantize the same recipe with N Hadamard sign seeds; score each on selection set A (base-correct items disjoint from calibration; metric = flip rate plus tail term, e.g. sum over items of exp(-m/ tau)*flip so low-margin flips count more); confirm on dev B. Expected: 4.3% -> ~3.2-3.5% (gain ~1.0-1.3 pt from the +-2 pt draw spread, section 3). Not enough alone but stacks with everything else and is ~free. Cost: N x quantizer time (32B on H100 per draw is the dominant cost; measured earlier: ~$2-4/draw on Modal [H, check RUNS.md]), roughly $30-60 for N=12 at 32B; 14B 3-4x cheaper. Legality: low-gray (hyper-parameter selection, exam untouched). Also settles whether the draw noise is real.
2. **GPTAQ-style asymmetric calibration, with self-distilled calibration texts in the task prompt format (class-balanced).** What: switch the Hessian residual to target full-precision layer outputs given quantized upstream (https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02692), calibrate on base-generated + task-format INJ/NEWS-style prompts balanced across S1/S3 and across labels (exclude the unseen-source block, and exclude exam/dev-eval items). Expected: 15-30% relative reduction in margin perturbation sigma, which by the tail argument (section 2.2) gives 4.3% -> ~2.8-3.3%; larger gain on deep stacks (405B) because it targets accumulation [H; GPTAQ reports gains at 2-4 bit in perplexity/zero-shot, I did not verify magnitudes]. Cost: a code change of ~20 lines plus one extra forward pass per layer; compute ~1.3-1.5x a GPTQ run, about the same dollars as #1 per draw. Legality: low (closed form, no training, no shipped extras).
3. **Fisher / logit-margin-weighted Hessian and codebook (decision-aware objective).** What: weight calibration tokens by gradient/Fisher of the base's label-token log-margin (diagonal Fisher per output channel, as in SqueezeLLM/LeanQuant/C-PTQ) so reconstruction error is concentrated where it moves the decision; emphasise low-margin and minority-class items (weight w = 1/ (1+margin) style). Expected: 10-25% sigma reduction on the decision direction, with the biggest effect on the failing cells (S3_injection, Technology, S2_benign) because their margins are the small ones; combined with #2 target ~2.3-2.8%. Risk: Fisher from <=1-2k samples is noisy; over-weighting few items overfits them (calibration-disjoint check required). Cost: one backward pass over calibration data per layer set (needs 32B backward memory: use CPU-offload / per-layer; 1 H100 for a few hours, ~$15-30). Legality: Gauss-Newton weights are pure math on calibration data; gradients are used as sensitivities not for updates: low-gray (state it explicitly in the repro).
4. **Per-layer / per-tensor bit allocation by margin-weighted sensitivity at fixed total bytes.** What: measure, per layer and per tensor type (down_proj, v_proj, o_proj, lm_head neighbours, last 4 layers, first layer), the KL-to-base (or margin loss) increase when only that tensor goes from the working bpw to a lower one; solve a knapsack (greedy) to move bits to sensitive tensors (known: last layers, embedding-adjacent and down_proj are sensitive [K]) with total at 9.6-10.0%. Expected: 10-20% sigma reduction, 4.3% -> ~3.3-3.8% alone, ~2% territory only stacked with #1-3. Cost: sensitivity sweep = one forward per (tensor, bit) on a small set; ~2-4 GPU hours, $10-25 at 14B, 2x at 32B. Legality: low (hyper-parameter allocation, ships the same format). Check the headers/side-info bytes of mixed precision count against the 10% cap.
5. **Learned rounding (AdaRound/BRECQ-style, block-wise, KL-to-base objective), only after legality clearance.** What: per block, optimise the up/down rounding mask on a fixed grid to minimise KL/MSE vs the *base block output* on calibration text (no labels, no new parameters). Expected: the strongest single lever: 20-40% reconstruction error reduction over GPTQ at 2-3 bit in vision/LLM reports [K, e.g. AdaRound/BRECQ and later LLM follow-ups such as AutoRound/SignRound]; guess 4.3% -> ~2.0-2.8% alone at 32B, possibly <2% stacked with #1-4. Cost: block-wise optimisation, a few hundred to a thousand steps per block: ~6-12 H100-hours for 32B, $25-60; lower for 14B. Legality: gray / medium-high (SGD on data though no weights are added); do not build on it unless a maintainer reply (open an issue; the deadline is 2026-11-12 17:05 UTC) or we accept the risk. Rank 5 only for legality, rank 1 by expected effect.

Not ranked (low expected value or illegal): post-hoc bias/norm-statistics correction (training-free; per-item effect is mostly already captured by GPTAQ's compensation; the potentially valuable variant is a class-prior shift, but that is a class-conditional bias fitted to labels, which risks rule 4); OmniQuant (trains scales); PV-tuning/KD (illegal); logit ensembling (size).

## 5. Heuristics to cut 4.3% -> 2% (summary)
- Aim for ~30% lower logit-margin perturbation; the Gaussian-tail argument says that is about a 2x flip reduction. No single trick gives that; plan to stack #2 + #3 + #4 (+#1 on top), with #5 as the optional high-effect/high-risk add-on.
- Measure flips with generation, class-conditionally, and with the **margin-weighted** tail score, so selection and ablations have lower variance than raw flip counts.
- Check **mu** (mean margin shift per class) of the quantized vs base before and after each change; if one class drifts, fix with class-balanced calibration, not with global bias.
- Always use >=3 draws; a 1-pt improvement under +-2 pt draw noise is not evidence without best-of-N-aware protocol (section 3).
- Honest expectation: even stacked, 14B probably stays above 2% flips (14B->32B alone moves retention 4-20 pt); the realistic win zone is 32B at 10.0% with all of #1-#4, and the decisive check is the second-source block and the weakest class cell (rule 2 per-class 95%), not the overall number.
